2-minute idea

An Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) burns fuel inside the engine cylinder. Hot gas expands and pushes the piston. That push turns the crankshaft — the same idea as a scooter, a tractor, or a genset.

A steam locomotive is an External Combustion Engine (ECE): fuel burns in a boiler, outside the working cylinder. Steam then goes to the engine. ICE is compact. ECE can use almost any fuel, but it is bulky.

Diploma exam language: heat is released by combustion of the charge inside the cylinder, and part of that heat is converted into mechanical work on the piston.

5 practice questions

If you still don’t get it

  • Mistake: “Diesel engine has a spark plug.” — CI engines ignite by compression heat, not a spark.
  • Mistake: Counting 2-stroke as “two cylinders.” — Stroke means piston travel, not number of cylinders.
  • Mistake: Writing compression ratio as D/L. — r = (Vs + Vc) / Vc.

Extra example: a 100 cc scooter engine means one cylinder swept volume is about 100 cm³. If clearance volume is 12.5 cm³, r = (100+12.5)/12.5 = 9.
