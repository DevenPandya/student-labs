Octane and cetane numbers

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · RON · MON · Knock · Ignition delay

What you should be able to do

  • Define octane number and name the reference fuels (iso-octane = 100, n-heptane = 0).
  • Tell RON from MON, and link octane number to knock and compression ratio.
  • Define cetane number, name its reference fuels, and link it to ignition delay and diesel knock.
  • Explain why a good petrol is a poor diesel fuel, and the reverse.

2-minute idea

A petrol engine lights the charge with a spark. The fuel should burn only when that flame arrives. If the last pocket — the end gas — explodes on its own, the engine knocks (detonation). Octane number measures resistance to that knock. The reference fuels are iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane) = 100 and n-heptane = 0. In a reference blend the octane number equals the percent of iso-octane by volume.

A diesel engine has no spark. It sprays fuel into hot compressed air. Ignition delay is the wait between the start of injection and the start of burning. A long wait lets fuel pile up; then it burns in a rush and the engine knocks (diesel knock). Cetane number measures how short that wait is. Cetane (n-hexadecane) = 100. The old zero was alpha-methylnaphthalene. On the modern scale the low reference is heptamethylnonane, set at 15.

The two scales point opposite ways. High octane means hard to auto-ignite: right for petrol, wrong for diesel. High cetane means easy to auto-ignite: right for diesel, wrong for petrol. A higher compression ratio heats the petrol end gas more, so knock comes sooner and a higher octane number is required. Higher octane is not extra energy in the fuel. It lets the designer use a higher compression ratio without detonation, and that higher ratio is what improves efficiency.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “Higher octane means more power or more km per litre.” — Octane is knock resistance, not calorific value. Extra octane above what the engine needs does not add energy. Octane that is too low causes knock, and a knock sensor may retard the spark, which does waste power.
  • Mistake: “Petrol knock and diesel knock are the same fault.” — Petrol knock is the end gas exploding too soon. Diesel knock is a long ignition delay, then a sudden burn. The fix is opposite: raise octane for petrol, raise cetane for diesel.
  • Mistake: “MON is the bigger number, and Indian pumps print MON.” — MON is the harsher test, so it is usually lower than RON. Pumps in India label RON (regular 91, premium often 95 or higher).
  • Mistake: “Octane number 100 means cetane number 100.” — The two 100s are different liquids. iso-Octane resists auto-ignition, so it is a poor diesel fuel. Cetane ignites readily, so it is a poor petrol.
