Exhaust system

What you should be able to do

  • List the jobs of the exhaust system (not only “reduce noise”).
  • Name the path: port/valve → manifold → pipe → silencer → tail pipe.
  • Name two or three silencer types used in diploma books.
  • Explain why high back pressure is bad.

2-minute idea

Burnt gas must leave quickly. If the pipe is too tight or the silencer is clogged, pressure just outside the exhaust valve stays high. The piston then wastes work pushing gas out. That leftover pressure is back pressure.

The silencer must cut noise without choking the engine. A good design uses expansion, baffles, or absorption so the pulse spreads out instead of slamming into one wall.

Path of the gas

Exhaust valve / port

→ catalytic converter (modern vehicles)
→ silencer / muffler
→ tail pipe → atmosphere

Other jobs besides noise

  • Lead hot gas away from the driver and from wooden bodywork.
  • Give a place for a catalyst or a diesel particulate filter on modern engines.
  • On turbo engines, feed the turbine first (next kit) before the silencer.

5 practice questions

If you still don’t get it

  • Mistake: “Silencer increases engine power.” — It always adds some restriction. A blocked silencer cuts power.
  • Mistake: “Back pressure is useful like compression.” — Compression is inside the cylinder on purpose. Back pressure is leftover resistance in the exhaust.
  • Mistake: “Manifold is the same as silencer.” — Manifold joins the ports. Silencer is the can that kills noise.
