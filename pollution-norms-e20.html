Pollution, emission norms and E-20

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · CO · HC · NOx · PM · BS-VI · E20

What you should be able to do

  • Name CO, HC, NOx, PM and CO2, and say how each one forms.
  • Contrast a petrol (SI) exhaust with a diesel (CI) exhaust.
  • Match the cleanup: three-way catalyst, lambda sensor, EGR, DPF, SCR / AdBlue.
  • State the BS-IV to BS-VI jump, and what E20 is, with one gain and one limit.

2-minute idea

CO forms when oxygen is short or the flame dies before carbon becomes CO2 — a rich mixture, or a flame quenched on a cold wall. HC is fuel that never burned: crevices, a quench layer, a misfire, a very rich or a very lean mix. NOx forms when nitrogen from the air meets oxygen in a hot flame. It peaks slightly lean of stoichiometric, where the flame is still hot and some oxygen is left. PM (soot) forms in a fuel-rich pocket with almost no oxygen — the core of a diesel spray. CO2 is complete combustion. It is the greenhouse gas, not a poison like CO. A catalyst does not remove it. Burning less fuel does.

A petrol engine stays near stoichiometric, so CO, HC and NOx all matter. A three-way catalyst can cut all three, but only while a lambda sensor holds lambda near 1. A diesel runs lean. CO and HC are lower, but NOx and soot are the problem. Excess air stops a three-way catalyst from reducing NOx. Diesels use EGR to cool the peak flame, a DPF to trap soot, and many BS-VI diesels add SCR with AdBlue (urea solution, about 32.5% urea) to turn NOx into nitrogen and water.

India skipped BS-V. From 1 April 2020, new vehicles and the fuel at the pump went from BS-IV to BS-VI. Sulphur in petrol and diesel fell from 50 ppm to 10 ppm, so catalysts and filters are not poisoned. For light passenger cars, diesel NOx fell from 0.25 g/km to 0.08 g/km and diesel PM from 0.025 g/km to 0.0045 g/km. E20 is petrol with 20% ethanol by volume. India brought that blending target forward from 2030 to 2025. Ethanol brings oxygen and octane, so CO tends to fall and less crude oil is imported. It has less energy per litre, and some older hoses and seals were not made for it.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “The catalytic converter removes CO2.” — A three-way catalyst turns CO into CO2. Tailpipe CO2 falls only when the engine burns less fuel.
  • Mistake: “A three-way catalyst cleans a diesel the same way.” — A diesel runs lean. The catalyst can oxidize CO and HC, but it cannot reduce NOx in excess air. Diesels need EGR and SCR.
  • Mistake: “Pour AdBlue into the diesel tank.” — AdBlue is a urea solution sprayed into the exhaust for SCR. It is not a fuel. In the tank it can damage the injection system.
  • Mistake: “E20 has no downside, and the tailpipe CO2 is zero.” — Energy per litre is lower, so an untuned engine can lose some km per litre. Older hoses and seals may not tolerate the ethanol. The carbon in the fuel still leaves as CO2; the climate gain is the crop and the petrol that was not imported, not a zero on the meter.
