Air-conditioning processes

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Sensible heat · Coil · Bypass factor · Mixing

What you should be able to do

  • Describe sensible heating, sensible cooling, and cooling with dehumidification on the chart.
  • State what happens to DBT and specific humidity in evaporative cooling and in chemical dehumidification.
  • Calculate bypass factor and contact factor, and the mixture state of two air streams.
  • Split coil heat into a sensible part and a latent part, and write the sensible heat factor.

2-minute idea

An air-conditioning process is a path on the psychrometric chart from lesson 4.1. Sensible heating and sensible cooling move horizontally: specific humidity ω stays the same, so the dew point stays the same. Heating raises DBT and lowers relative humidity. Cooling does the opposite, but only while the coil surface stays above the dew point. No water condenses on a purely sensible path.

If the coil is colder than the dew point, vapour condenses. The path slopes down and left: DBT falls and ω falls. That is cooling and dehumidification. The coil surface temperature on the chart is the apparatus dew point (ADP). Some air sneaks through without touching the coil. The bypass factor is BF = (t2 − tADP) / (t1 − tADP). The same fraction applies to ω. Contact factor = 1 − BF. Heat removed per kg of dry air is h1 − h2. Water removed is ω1 − ω2. Sensible heat factor SHF = sensible heat / total heat. SHF = 1 for a purely sensible process.

Evaporative cooling (an air washer with recirculated water) follows a nearly constant wet-bulb line. DBT falls, ω rises, and enthalpy hardly changes. Humidifying efficiency = (t1 − t2) / (t1 − tWBT). Heating and humidification adds both heat and moisture, so DBT and ω rise. Chemical dehumidification (silica gel or a liquid absorbent) removes moisture and usually warms the air, because the latent heat is released: ω falls and DBT rises.

Mixing two streams is a straight line on the chart. The mixture point is closer to the larger stream. With m as kg of dry air, ω3 = (m1ω1 + m2ω2) / (m1 + m2) and h3 = (m1h1 + m2h2) / (m1 + m2). Enthalpy here is the same approximation as lesson 4.1: h ≈ 1.005 t + ω(2500 + 1.88 t) kJ/kg dry air. Saturation pressure is the same Magnus approximation.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “Sensible cooling always produces condensate.” — Condensate appears only when the surface is below the dew point of the entering air. Above the dew point, ω stays constant.
  • Mistake: “Bypass factor is (t1 − t2) / (t1 − tADP).” — That ratio is the contact factor, 1 − BF. Bypass factor is the air that missed the coil: (t2 − tADP) / (t1 − tADP).
  • Mistake: “Two equal-looking streams always mix at the midpoint, whatever their flow rates.” — The mixture is mass-weighted. Double the flow of stream 1 and the mixture moves toward state 1.
  • Mistake: “An evaporative cooler dries the air because the dry bulb falls.” — The dry bulb falls, but water evaporates into the air, so ω rises. The wet-bulb temperature stays nearly the same.
