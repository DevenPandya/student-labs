Lubrication system

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Splash · Pressure · Petroil

What you should be able to do

  • List the jobs of lubricating oil (not only “reduce friction”).
  • Compare splash, pressure-feed, and petroil systems.
  • Name pump, filter, gallery, relief valve in a wet-sump engine.
  • State two important oil properties used in exams (viscosity, VI).

2-minute idea

Metal sliding on metal without a film will weld and tear. Oil keeps a thin film between piston and liner, and between journal and bearing. That film also carries heat away, seals the piston rings, and washes grit into the filter.

Cooling removes heat from the jackets. Lubrication protects the rubbing surfaces. They work together. Oil is not a substitute for coolant.

Properties you must name

      
    
        
  • Viscosity — resistance to flow. Too thin: film breaks. Too thick: hard starting, extra drag.
    • 
        
  • Viscosity index (VI) — how little viscosity changes with temperature. High VI is better for Gujarat heat + cold start.
    • 
        
  • Flash point — lowest temp at which vapours ignite. Higher is safer in a hot sump.
    • 
        
  • Pour point — lowest temp at which oil still pours. Matters in winter, less in most of Gujarat.
    • 
      

      
SAE 20W-40 means the oil behaves like SAE 20 when cold and SAE 40 when hot (multigrade).

If you still don’t get it

      
    
        
  • Mistake: “Lubrication only reduces friction.” — Also cools, seals, cleans, and damps.
    • 
        
  • Mistake: “Petroil is used in all diesel trucks.” — Petroil is the 2-stroke petrol mix. Diesels use a separate oil circuit.
    • 
        
  • Mistake: “Relief valve increases oil pressure.” — It limits pressure by dumping extra oil back to the sump.
    • 
      

      
Exam path for pressure lubrication: sump → strainer → pump → filter → main gallery → bearings / cam / rockers → drain back to sump.
