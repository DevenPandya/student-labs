VCRS performance and operating conditions

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Evaporator · Condenser · COP · Capacity

What you should be able to do

  • Say how evaporator temperature and condenser temperature change COP and capacity.
  • Use the reversed Carnot COP, in kelvin, as the upper limit and the direction of change.
  • Explain why a fixed reciprocating compressor pumps less mass when the pressure ratio rises.
  • Write the clearance volumetric efficiency and say what raises or lowers it.

2-minute idea

The COP of a vapour compression plant is not a nameplate constant. It follows the two temperatures. The ceiling is the reversed Carnot value from lesson 3.1: COP = T_L / (T_H − T_L), with both temperatures in kelvin (°C + 273). A colder evaporator or a hotter condenser lowers that ceiling. A real plant sits below the ceiling, but it moves in the same direction.

Colder evaporator means lower suction pressure. The pressure ratio p_discharge / p_suction rises, so compressor work per kilogram rises. Suction vapour is also less dense, and volumetric efficiency falls, so a fixed-speed reciprocating compressor delivers less mass. Refrigerating capacity therefore falls even though the room needs more cooling. Do not run the evaporator colder than the product requires.

A hotter condenser means higher discharge pressure. Liquid leaving the condenser has a higher enthalpy, so more of it flashes in the expansion valve and the refrigerating effect per kilogram falls. Work per kilogram rises. COP and capacity both fall. A dirty condenser, a blocked fan, or a hot day does this. Keep the condenser clean and well cooled.

For a reciprocating compressor at fixed speed and fixed condenser temperature, shaft power is not greatest at the coldest evaporator. It peaks at an intermediate suction temperature, then falls again when so little mass is pumped. COP and capacity keep falling as the evaporator gets colder. Subcooling still helps COP (lesson 3.2). Heat gained by the suction pipe outside the cold room is not useful refrigeration, and it usually adds compressor work.

Clearance volumetric efficiency

η_vol = 1 − C [(p_d / p_s)^(1/n) − 1]. C is clearance volume / swept volume. n is the expansion index you choose for the clearance gas. This ignores leakage and suction heating, so a real compressor is lower. It is not a property of one named refrigerant.

Shaft power of a fixed-speed reciprocating compressor, at one condenser temperature, peaks at an intermediate evaporator temperature. The peak temperature depends on the machine. COP and capacity do not peak there: both are worse when the evaporator is colder.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “A colder evaporator gives a better COP, because the plant is refrigerating harder.” — COP is cooling divided by work. A colder evaporator raises the work and lowers the Carnot ceiling. Capacity of a given compressor falls too.
  • Mistake: “Use °C in T_L / (T_H − T_L).” — Add 273. −20°C is 253 K. −10°C and 40°C give 263 / 50 = 5.26. −20°C and 40°C give 253 / 60 = 4.22.
  • Mistake: “A hot, dirty condenser increases capacity.” — It raises condenser temperature and pressure. Refrigerating effect per kilogram falls, work rises, COP and capacity fall.
  • Mistake: “Higher discharge pressure raises volumetric efficiency.” — It raises the pressure ratio. η_vol = 1 − C [(p_d / p_s)^(1/n) − 1] gets smaller. Less vapour is drawn in each stroke.
