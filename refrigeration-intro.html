Introduction to refrigeration

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · TR · COP · Reversed Carnot

What you should be able to do

  • Say what refrigeration is, and why heat will not flow from cold to hot by itself.
  • Define the ton of refrigeration and convert TR to kJ/min and kW.
  • Write COP of a refrigerator and of a heat pump, and the link COP_HP = COP_R + 1.
  • Calculate reversed Carnot COP in kelvin, and relative COP.

2-minute idea

Refrigeration removes heat from a space and keeps that space colder than its surroundings. A household fridge, a cold store, an ice plant and a water cooler all do this. Heat does not flow from the cold space to the hotter air outside on its own. The Clausius statement of the second law says a machine cannot, unaided, move heat from a colder body to a hotter body. Work has to be supplied (the compressor). For a cycle, the heat rejected at the hot side is the heat taken from the cold side plus that work: Q_H = Q_L + W.

A refrigerator is judged by the cooling. COP = refrigerating effect / work input = Q_L / W. A heat pump is the same kind of machine judged by the heating, so its COP = Q_H / W. Because Q_H = Q_L + W, COP_HP = COP_R + 1. COP is not a heat-engine efficiency. It is often greater than 1, and that is allowed, because the effect can be larger than the work you put in.

The practical unit is the ton of refrigeration. 1 TR is the rate of cooling that freezes 1 short ton of water (2000 lb, about 907 kg — not the 1000 kg metric tonne) already at 0°C into ice at 0°C in 24 hours. Latent heat of ice is about 335 kJ/kg, so 907 × 335 kJ in 24 hours is 211 kJ/min. Divide by 60: 211 kJ/min = 3.52 kW. Many problem sheets round 1 TR to 3.5 kW.

No real plant beats a reversed Carnot refrigerator between the same two temperatures. Temperatures must be in kelvin (°C + 273). COP_R = T_L / (T_H − T_L). The Carnot heat pump is T_H / (T_H − T_L), which is the same as COP_R + 1. Relative COP = actual COP / Carnot COP. For a real machine it is less than 1.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “Put °C straight into the Carnot COP.” — The ratio uses absolute temperature. Add 273. −10°C is 263 K, not −10 K.
  • Mistake: “COP cannot be more than 1.” — That limit is for a heat-engine efficiency (work out / heat in). A refrigerator COP is cooling / work. 2 or 3 is normal and does not break the second law.
  • Mistake: “1 TR = 211 kW, or 3.5 kJ/min.” — 1 TR = 211 kJ/min, which is about 3.5 kW. The 211 and the 3.5 do not share a unit.
  • Mistake: “The ton in TR is 1000 kg, and COP_HP = COP_R − 1.” — The ton is the short ton, about 907 kg. And because the hot side receives Q_L plus the work, the heat-pump COP is the refrigerator COP plus 1, not minus 1.
