Exhaust-gas analysis

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Orsat · NDIR · FID · PUC

What you should be able to do

  • Say why exhaust is analysed, and name CO, CO2, HC, O2 and NOx with their units.
  • Calculate CO2, O2 and CO from Orsat readings, and name the three reagents in order.
  • Match NDIR, FID, chemiluminescence, the lambda sensor and a smoke meter to the right job.
  • Read high CO, high O2 and high HC, and contrast a petrol PUC test with a diesel one.

2-minute idea

Exhaust gas answers three questions: did the fuel burn properly, was the mixture rich or lean, and would the vehicle pass a pollution check. CO, CO2 and O2 are quoted in percent by volume. HC and NOx are small, so they are quoted in ppm (parts per million). 1% by volume = 10,000 ppm.

An Orsat apparatus is the classroom wet test, on a dry sample (water vapour is condensed first). Usually 100 ml is bubbled through potassium hydroxide (KOH), also called caustic potash, which absorbs CO2, then alkaline pyrogallol which absorbs O2, then ammoniacal cuprous chloride which absorbs CO. The volume drop is that gas. On a 100 ml sample, millilitres absorbed = percent. Gas left is counted as nitrogen. Pyrogallol would also absorb CO2, so KOH must come first. Orsat does not measure HC or NOx.

A modern analyser splits the jobs. NDIR (non-dispersive infrared) reads CO, CO2 and HC, because each gas absorbs infrared at its own wavelengths. FID (flame ionisation detector) is the better lab method for total HC: a hydrogen flame turns hydrocarbons into ions. NOx is measured by chemiluminescence — nitric oxide reacts with ozone and the light given off is the measure. A zirconia lambda sensor only reports oxygen, as a voltage: about 0.9 V when rich (little O2) and about 0.1 V when lean. Diesel smoke is not a gas percentage. A Bosch meter stains filter paper (a higher number is darker, on a scale from 0). A Hartridge meter reads how much light the smoke blocks, from 0 (clear) to 100.

How to read a petrol result: high CO means rich; high O2 means lean, or air leaking into the exhaust; high HC means fuel did not burn (misfire, weak spark, or a very poor mixture). High CO2 with low CO and low O2 is a good burn near stoichiometric. A healthy petrol idle often reaches about 14 to 15% CO2 — a guide, not a legal limit. In India a petrol PUC check measures idle CO and HC. A diesel PUC check is free-acceleration smoke opacity. The pass limit depends on the vehicle and is printed on the slip.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “Orsat measures HC in ppm.” — Orsat absorbs only CO2, O2 and CO, on a dry volume basis. HC needs an FID or NDIR. NOx needs chemiluminescence.
  • Mistake: “Use pyrogallol first.” — Alkaline pyrogallol absorbs oxygen and would also take CO2. KOH must remove CO2 before the oxygen step, or the CO2 reading is lost.
  • Mistake: “High CO2 is bad, like high CO.” — CO is incomplete combustion and is poisonous. CO2 is what you get when carbon does burn completely. High CO2 with low CO is a good sign.
  • Mistake: “A diesel smoke reading is a CO percentage.” — Smoke is soot blocking light (Hartridge / opacity) or darkening paper (Bosch). A petrol PUC slip is CO and HC. Do not mix the units: percent, ppm, and opacity are different.
