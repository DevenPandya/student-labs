Vapour compression cycle (VCRS)

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · p-h · COP · Subcooling · Superheat

What you should be able to do

  • Name the four components and the job of each.
  • Sketch the standard cycle on p-h and T-s diagrams.
  • Find refrigerating effect, compressor work and COP from enthalpies.
  • Say what liquid subcooling and suction superheat change.

2-minute idea

The vapour compression system is the usual refrigeration plant. Refrigerant vapour is compressed, turned to liquid in the condenser, throttled to a low pressure, and boiled in the evaporator. The four parts, in flow order, are compressor → condenser → expansion device → evaporator, then back to the compressor. The expansion device is a throttle valve or, on a small fridge, a capillary tube. It does no shaft work.

On the p-h diagram (pressure against enthalpy) the ideal cycle is easy to read, because heat and work per kilogram are horizontal distances. 1–2 compression: pressure and enthalpy rise. Ideal compression is isentropic. 2–3 condenser: constant high pressure, enthalpy falls as the vapour cools and condenses. 3–4 throttle: vertical line, h3 = h4. 4–1 evaporator: constant low pressure, enthalpy rises as liquid boils. On the T-s diagram, isentropic compression is a vertical line up into superheat, condensation is a horizontal line at the hot temperature, evaporation is a horizontal line at the cold temperature, and throttling is not vertical — entropy increases.

Per kilogram: refrigerating effect q_L = h1 − h4 = h1 − h3, compressor work w = h2 − h1, heat rejected q_H = h2 − h3. Check: q_H = q_L + w. COP = (h1 − h3) / (h2 − h1). Enthalpies are kJ/kg, so q and w are kJ/kg.

Subcooling (undercooling) cools the liquid below the saturation temperature at condenser pressure. h3 falls, so h4 falls, the refrigerating effect rises, and compressor work is unchanged if the suction state is unchanged. COP rises, and less flash gas forms after the valve. Superheating the suction vapour makes sure the compressor gets dry vapour. Liquid droplets (wet compression) can damage it. If that extra heat is picked up outside the cold room, it is not useful refrigeration, and the compressor work usually rises.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “The throttle is a vertical line on the T-s diagram.” — A vertical line on T-s is isentropic compression. The throttle is vertical on the p-h diagram, because h3 = h4. On T-s, throttling increases entropy.
  • Mistake: “COP = (h2 − h1) / (h1 − h3).” — That is work divided by cooling, which is 1/COP. Cooling is on top: (h1 − h3) / (h2 − h1).
  • Mistake: “Subcooling reduces the refrigerating effect.” — A lower h3 lowers h4. The evaporator can then add more enthalpy per kilogram, so q_L increases. Work stays the same if the suction state is the same, so COP rises.
  • Mistake: “A little liquid in the compressor is useful.” — Wet compression can damage the valves and wash out the oil. Suction superheat is there to keep the vapour dry.
