Psychrometry and the chart

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · DBT · WBT · DPT · Humidity

What you should be able to do

  • Distinguish dry air from moist air, and state Dalton’s law.
  • Define DBT, WBT, DPT and wet-bulb depression, and put them in order.
  • Use ω = 0.622 pv / (p − pv) and φ = pv / ps, and separate φ from degree of saturation.
  • Read DBT, specific humidity and dew point on a psychrometric chart.

2-minute idea

Dry air is air with no water vapour. Moist air is dry air plus water vapour. In ordinary air conditioning the vapour is superheated: the air is unsaturated. Dalton’s law: the total pressure is the sum of the partial pressures, p = pa + pv. Each gas behaves as if it filled the whole volume at the mixture temperature.

DBT (dry-bulb temperature) is the ordinary thermometer temperature. WBT (wet-bulb temperature) is the temperature of a thermometer with a wet wick in moving air. Evaporation cools the wick. DPT (dew-point temperature) is the temperature at which the air would become saturated if cooled at constant pressure and constant humidity. For unsaturated air, DPT < WBT < DBT. On saturated air all three are equal. Wet-bulb depression is DBT − WBT. It is zero when the air is saturated, and larger when the air is drier.

Specific humidity (humidity ratio) ω is kilograms of water vapour per kilogram of dry air. ω = 0.622 pv / (p − pv). The 0.622 is the ratio of the molecular weight of water (18.015) to that of dry air (about 28.97). Relative humidity φ = pv / ps, where ps is the saturation pressure of water at the DBT. Degree of saturation μ = ω / ωs. It equals φ only when the air is saturated. Otherwise μ is a little smaller: μ = φ (p − ps) / (p − pv). Enthalpy of moist air, per kg of dry air, with the usual 0°C reference, is h ≈ 1.005 t + ω (2500 + 1.88 t) kJ/kg dry air. Here 1.005 is cp of dry air, 2500 kJ/kg is the latent heat at 0°C, and 1.88 is cp of water vapour.

On a psychrometric chart the horizontal axis is DBT. Specific humidity increases upward. The left-hand curve is saturation (φ = 100%). Other curves are constant relative humidity. Horizontal lines are constant ω and constant dew point: from the state point, go left to the saturation curve and read the dew-point temperature. Wet-bulb lines slope down to the right and are nearly the constant-enthalpy lines. Follow one to the saturation curve to read WBT. The state is fixed by any two independent properties, often DBT and RH, or DBT and WBT.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “Relative humidity and degree of saturation are the same.” — φ = pv / ps. μ = ω / ωs. They are both 1 only for saturated air. Otherwise μ = φ (p − ps) / (p − pv), which is a little smaller than φ.
  • Mistake: “Specific humidity is kilograms of vapour per kilogram of moist air.” — ω is per kilogram of dry air. The formula 0.622 pv / (p − pv) is that ratio.
  • Mistake: “Dew point is found by moving vertically on the chart.” — A vertical line is constant DBT. Constant humidity, and therefore constant dew point, is a horizontal line. Meet the saturation curve and read the temperature.
  • Mistake: “The enthalpy formula is per kilogram of moist air, and wet-bulb depression is DBT − DPT.” — h ≈ 1.005 t + ω(2500 + 1.88 t) is per kg of dry air. Wet-bulb depression is DBT − WBT, not DBT − DPT.
