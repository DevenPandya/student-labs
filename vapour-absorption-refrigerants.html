Vapour absorption (VARS) and refrigerants

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Aqua-ammonia · LiBr · ASHRAE number · ODP

What you should be able to do

  • Describe a vapour absorption plant and write its COP as cooling divided by heat input.
  • Tell aqua-ammonia from LiBr-water, and say what the third fluid does in an Electrolux fridge.
  • Read a simple ASHRAE number, including 700 plus molecular weight for inorganics.
  • Separate ozone depletion (Montreal, 1987) from the HFC phasedown (Kigali, 2016).

2-minute idea

A vapour absorption plant still has a condenser, an expansion device and an evaporator. It does not use a compressor. Vapour from the evaporator is absorbed into a liquid. A small pump sends that strong solution to a generator, where heat drives the refrigerant vapour off again. The weak solution returns to the absorber. Pump work is small because a liquid is being pumped, not a vapour. The diploma COP is refrigerating effect / heat supplied to the generator. Do not compare that number with a VCRS COP: one divides by heat, the other by compressor work. Absorption is chosen when waste heat, steam or a gas flame is cheaper than electric compressor work.

In aqua-ammonia, ammonia is the refrigerant and water is the absorbent. Ammonia can boil below 0°C, so this pair can freeze. An analyser and a rectifier strip water out of the vapour before the condenser. In a LiBr-water plant, water is the refrigerant and lithium bromide is the absorbent. It is used for air conditioning, not ice: the evaporator cannot go below 0°C, and the plant runs under a strong vacuum. The Electrolux (three-fluid) domestic fridge uses ammonia, water and hydrogen. There is no pump. Total pressure is about the same around the loop. Hydrogen in the evaporator lowers the partial pressure of ammonia, so ammonia can evaporate at a low temperature.

The ASHRAE number for a saturated halocarbon reads from the right. Last digit = fluorine atoms. Next digit = hydrogen atoms + 1. The digit after that = carbon atoms − 1, and it is omitted when it is zero. Chlorine fills the remaining bonds: for a saturated molecule, Cl = 2C + 2 − H − F. A final letter marks the isomer. Worked example, R-22: F = 2, H = 2 − 1 = 1, C = 1, Cl = 4 − 1 − 2 = 1, so CHClF2. Inorganic refrigerants are 700 + molecular weight: ammonia 17 so R-717, water 18 so R-718, carbon dioxide 44 so R-744.

A good refrigerant has a high latent heat, working pressures that are neither a deep vacuum nor extreme, a freezing point below the evaporator temperature, and chemical stability. It should also spare the ozone layer and trap little heat. ODP (ozone depletion potential) uses R-11 as 1. CFCs such as R-12 have high ODP. R-22, an HCFC, still has chlorine; its ODP is small but not zero, about 0.055. R-134a, an HFC, has ODP = 0 and a high GWP. GWP uses CO2 as 1. The Montreal Protocol (1987) phases out ozone-depleting substances. The Kigali Amendment (2016) is the later phasedown of high-GWP HFCs. Countries are on different timetables. Ammonia, CO2, and hydrocarbons such as R-600a have ODP = 0 and a very low GWP. Ammonia is toxic and is not used with copper. R-600a is flammable.

Common mistakes

  • Mistake: “A VARS COP of less than 1 means the plant is worse than a VCRS COP of 4.” — The absorption COP divides by heat supplied. The compression COP divides by compressor work. They are not the same kind of number. Absorption earns its place when the heat is cheap or would otherwise be wasted.
  • Mistake: “In a LiBr plant, lithium bromide is the refrigerant.” — Water is the refrigerant. LiBr is the absorbent. That is why the machine cannot make ice.
  • Mistake: “R-134a destroys ozone, so Montreal already banned it with R-12.” — R-134a has ODP = 0. R-12 is a CFC phased out for ozone. R-134a is an HFC; Kigali is the later agreement on its high GWP.
  • Mistake: “R-22 means 22 fluorine atoms.” — The last digit is the fluorine count, so F = 2. The tens digit says hydrogen + 1, so H = 1. One carbon, and the remaining bond is chlorine: CHClF2.
