Turbocharging

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Boost · Lag · Wastegate

What you should be able to do

  • Say why we force more air into the cylinder (more air → more fuel → more power from the same engine size).
  • Name the two wheels: exhaust turbine and intake compressor on one shaft.
  • Distinguish turbocharger from supercharger.
  • State what a wastegate and an intercooler do.

2-minute idea

A naturally aspirated engine drinks only atmosphere. A turbo uses energy that would have gone out of the tail pipe. Exhaust spins a turbine; that shaft spins a compressor; the compressor packs more air into the intake.

More trapped air means more oxygen. The engine can burn more fuel each cycle without growing the bore and stroke. That is why a small turbo diesel can pull like a larger NA engine.

Gas path

Exhaust manifold → turbine → (wastegate bypass) → silencer
Fresh air → filter → compressor → intercooler → inlet manifold

Limits you must mention

  • Turbo lag — delay after you open the throttle before boost arrives.
  • Overboost — too much pressure; wastegate dumps exhaust around the turbine.
  • Hot charge — compression heats the air; intercooler cools it so density stays high and knocking risk falls (SI).
  • Bearing / oil — the shaft spins at very high rpm and needs clean oil (link to kit 1.5).

5 practice questions

If you still don’t get it

  • Mistake: “Turbo is an electric fan on the radiator.” — It is a turbine + compressor in the gas path.
  • Mistake: “Supercharger is powered by exhaust.” — Supercharger is crank-driven. Turbo is exhaust-driven.
  • Mistake: “Wastegate increases boost without limit.” — It limits boost by bypassing the turbine.
