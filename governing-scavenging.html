Governing and scavenging

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Governor · 2-stroke scavenging

What you should be able to do

  • State why an engine needs a governor.
  • Describe a simple centrifugal governor in 4–5 lines.
  • Define scavenging and name three 2-stroke methods.
  • Say how valve overlap helps scavenging on a 4-stroke.

2-minute idea

Governing: Load on a generator or pump changes. Without control the engine would race when load falls and stall when load rises. A governor meters fuel (or throttle) so speed stays nearly constant.

Scavenging: Push burnt gas out and let fresh charge in. On a 2-stroke there is no separate exhaust stroke, so ports + incoming air (or mixture) must sweep the cylinder. On a 4-stroke a little of this happens during valve overlap.

Governor lab — load vs fuel

Imagine a diesel generator set at 1500 rpm. Drag the load. A working governor cuts fuel when load drops so the engine does not overspeed.

Words examiners like

  • Hunting — speed keeps swinging up and down around the set value.
  • Sensitiveness — how small a speed change is needed before the governor moves.
  • Scavenge efficiency — how much of the trapped gas is actually fresh charge.
  • Short-circuiting — fresh charge slips out of the exhaust port without filling the cylinder.

If you still don’t get it

  • Mistake: “Governor is the same as a flywheel.” — Flywheel stores energy for one cycle. Governor changes fuel when mean load changes.
  • Mistake: “Scavenging is only for 4-stroke petrol.” — The syllabus term is mainly the 2-stroke port story. 4-stroke overlap is a small extra point.
  • Mistake: “Uniflow needs a deflector piston.” — Deflector belongs to old cross-scavenge designs.
