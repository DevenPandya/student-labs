Cooling system

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · Air cooling · Water cooling · Thermostat

What you should be able to do

  • Give two reasons an IC engine must be cooled.
  • Compare air cooling and water (liquid) cooling.
  • Name the main parts of a pump–radiator system and the job of the thermostat.
  • State what happens if cooling is too little or too much.

2-minute idea

Only about 25–35% of the fuel’s heat becomes useful work. A large share goes to the cylinder walls. If that heat is not removed, the oil burns, the piston seizes, and valves warp.

Cooling must not be “maximum cold.” A slightly warm engine needs less viscous oil, better vaporisation, and fewer emissions. The thermostat holds jacket water near 80–90 °C in a typical liquid-cooled engine.

If cooling fails — or is excessive

  • Overheating: oil thins and burns, piston expansion → seizure, detonation, cracked head.
  • Overcooling: poor vaporisation, dilution of oil by unburnt fuel, more wear, more HC.

5 practice questions

If you still don’t get it

  • Mistake: “Thermostat cools the engine.” — It restricts flow until the engine is warm, then opens to the radiator.
  • Mistake: “Radiator makes cold water from nothing.” — It dumps heat to air passing through the core.
  • Mistake: “Air-cooled engines have a radiator.” — They have fins. Water-cooled engines have the radiator.

Exam sentence: water is pumped through jackets, picks up heat, goes to the radiator when the thermostat opens, and returns cooler to the pump.
