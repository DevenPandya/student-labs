Valve timing diagrams

Thermal Engineering-II · DI05019021 · 4-stroke SI / CI · GTU Diploma

What you should be able to do

  • Draw the theoretical 4-stroke valve timing circle (IVO, IVC, EVO, EVC at dead centres).
  • Draw a typical actual diagram with lead and lag angles.
  • Mark valve overlap and say why it is used.
  • Explain in 4–5 lines why actual timing is not the same as theoretical.

2-minute idea

The crankshaft turns 720° for one 4-stroke cycle (two revolutions). Valves must open and close at the right crank angle. A valve timing diagram is that story drawn on a circle.

Theoretical: inlet opens at TDC and closes at BDC. Exhaust opens at BDC and closes at TDC. Nice on paper. Too slow in a real engine.

Actual: inlet opens a little before TDC and closes a little after BDC. Exhaust opens before BDC and closes after TDC. Those extra degrees are called lead (early) and lag (late). Near TDC both valves are slightly open together — that band is valve overlap.

Typical actual angles (textbook 4-stroke SI)

Exam diagrams use values in this range. Your teacher may give slightly different numbers — write those in the answer book.

EventTheoreticalActual (typical)
IVO — inlet valve opensTDC10°–20° before TDC
IVC — inlet valve closesBDC30°–40° after BDC
EVO — exhaust valve opensBDC30°–50° before BDC
EVC — exhaust valve closesTDC8°–20° after TDC

This kit draws: IVO 15° bTDC, IVC 35° aBDC, EVO 40° bBDC, EVC 12° aTDC. Overlap = 15° + 12° = 27°.

Why actual ≠ theoretical

  • Valves need time to lift. They do not slam fully open at one exact degree.
  • Gas has inertia. Incoming charge keeps flowing after BDC, so IVC is delayed to fill the cylinder better (ram effect).
  • Early EVO starts blow-down so exhaust pressure drops before the piston begins the exhaust stroke — less pumping work.
  • Overlap lets outgoing exhaust help pull fresh charge in (scavenging), especially at higher speed.

CI engines use the same idea. Spark is replaced by injection timing (fuel injected a few degrees before TDC).

Try the effect of overlap

How to draw it in the exam (4-stroke)

  • Draw a circle. Mark TDC at the top, BDC at the bottom.
  • Mark 180° suction (TDC→BDC), 180° compression (BDC→TDC), 180° power (TDC→BDC), 180° exhaust (BDC→TDC).
  • Theoretical: put IVO and EVC on TDC, IVC and EVO on BDC.
  • Actual: move IVO left of TDC (before), IVC past BDC, EVO before BDC, EVC past TDC.
  • Shade or label the small TDC region where both valves are open — write “valve overlap”.
  • Write the degrees your question paper gives. Do not invent extra events.

5 practice questions

If you still don’t get it

  • Mistake: Drawing a 360° circle as one full 4-stroke cycle. One 4-stroke cycle is two crank revolutions = 720°.
  • Mistake: Putting IVC before BDC on the actual diagram. Actual IVC is after BDC (lag).
  • Mistake: Calling overlap “both valves closed.” Overlap = both valves open near TDC (end of exhaust + start of suction).

Extra check: if IVO = 15° bTDC and EVC = 10° aTDC, overlap = 25°.
